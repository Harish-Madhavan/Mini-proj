02 · Evidence vocabulary

Keep facts, hypotheses, and outcomes separate

ON-CHAIN RECORD

What a provider reports

Transaction IDs, outputs, values, timestamps, spend status, and graph links.

Check the provider and preserve its raw response where needed.

ANALYTICAL HYPOTHESIS

What patterns suggest

Change/payment candidates, ownership clusters, taint proportions, and risk scores.

Confidence is not a probability of ownership or wrongdoing.

REPORTED OUTCOME

What an investigator records

Request submitted, response received, or recovery reported with supporting reference.

The application does not independently confirm recovery.

Important: A verified flag means a record is marked verified in case data; it does not mean AegisTrace authenticated the source or proved who controls an address.
Transition: “These labels are the guardrails for every feature we’ll see next.”

06 · Taint analysis

Compare accounting assumptions

PROPORTIONATE / HAIRCUT

Carries a proportional taint share through mixed value.

FIFO

Allocates value by an assumed first-in-first-out ordering.

POISON

Marks all output value contaminated if any tainted input contributes.

Illustrative arithmetic—not a real case

Suppose 1 BTC of a 2 BTC input is marked tainted and the transaction pays 1.5 BTC to an output. A proportionate model would estimate 0.75 BTC tainted there. FIFO or poison can produce different allocations based on their assumptions.

Show: Tracing → taint controls/heatmap → switch accounting model. Point out that totals are model-dependent and not proof that specific satoshis can be identified on-chain.
Presenter cue: Never report the illustrative 0.75 BTC as a recovered or legally attributable amount.

08 · Linked cases

Find overlap—don’t overstate identity

How to demonstrate
  1. Open Linked cases.
  2. Load/select at least two investigation cases with an overlapping address.
  3. Inspect the shared-address relationship and its source graph.
  4. Record whether the overlap is meaningful or an alternative explanation.
Interpretation

A shared address may reflect a service, shared custody, a deposit point, or unrelated circumstances. A network edge or score ranks a lead for review; it does not prove one operator or criminal association.

Candidate linkage

09 · Risk triage

Prioritize review, not conclusions

Demonstrate
  1. Open Risk.
  2. Select a case or candidate.
  3. Inspect each dimension and contributing factors.
  4. Change a weight only to show how assumptions affect ordering.
Explain

Risk scores are configurable analytical prioritization. They are not statutory thresholds, a finding of wrongdoing, or calibrated probabilities of criminality.

Present the underlying evidence and counter-explanations beside any score.

Use this phrase: “This score tells us what to inspect next under these assumptions—not what a court or investigator should conclude.”

14 · Asset recovery

Turn trace context into a prioritized follow-up queue

Lead information
  • Attributed taint estimate and gross moved value.
  • Last observed transaction and destination type only when recorded evidence supports them.
  • Unspent/available amount only when chain or case data supports it.
  • Priority combines value, recency, evidence, and verified-contact records.
Action workflow
  1. Add a preservation or lawful information-request action.
  2. Attach a reference and follow-up date.
  3. Move through allowed action states.
  4. Record an outcome reference before marking recovery confirmed.

16 · Operational limits

What the demo should not promise

NOT PROVEN BY THE APP
  • Address ownership or real-world identity.
  • Criminal intent from transaction shape.
  • Legal admissibility or source authenticity.
  • Accuracy on unseen cases from one historic fixture.
REQUIRES INVESTIGATOR REVIEW
  • Alternative explanations and path completeness.
  • Provider response/source and attribution evidence.
  • Legal authority and service contact validity.
  • Recovery status and outcome documentation.
One-line caveat: “AegisTrace organizes public-chain observations and investigative hypotheses; it does not independently establish identity, intent, or legal conclusions.”
